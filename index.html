Four ways to get hot. Help us find out which one people would actually use.

Healthy adults aged 18–65 are invited to take part in a University of Brighton PhD study comparing three portable saunas with a hot bath, to understand how they could help people prepare for heatwaves.

Why this study?

Heatwaves are becoming more frequent in the UK and pose a high risk to health, especially as we get older. Repeated heating can help the body adapt to heat, and passive methods, which do not need exercise, may suit people more vulnerable to heat.

Most research so far has used hot water immersion in young, active men, such as athletes and military personnel. Much less is known about middle-aged and older adults, or women. Hot baths can also be difficult and less safe to use at home.

Portable saunas are cheaper and more practical, but we do not yet know whether they heat the body as much as a hot bath, or whether people find them comfortable enough to use.

What you could get from taking part

  • Try four different ways of passive heating in a supervised lab.
  • See how your own body responds to heat, including your core temperature and heart rate in each session, if you would like a copy.
  • Receive a short, plain-English summary of the findings when the study ends.
  • Help shape future advice on protecting people during heatwaves.

Your results are for interest only and are not a medical assessment. Each method is tried once, so your body is not expected to adapt to heat during this study.

The four heats

At each visit you try one method for a total of 45 minutes. In the saunas, you can step out for optional 5-minute breaks whenever you choose. The order is chosen at random.

A hotter number does not always feel hotter

Water and steam move heat into the body far faster than dry air, which is why a 40°C bath can heat you as much as a much hotter sauna. Finding out how each method compares is the point of this study.

The same in every session

Whichever method you are trying, a researcher stays with you, your core and skin temperature and heart rate are monitored live, and every 10 minutes we ask how hot and comfortable you feel. You can stop at any time.

Your visits

Each visit lasts around 2 hours. The first is longer, around 3 hours, because we start with consent, a health questionnaire and a few extra measurements.

  1. Hydration check

    You provide a small urine sample in private. If you are not well hydrated, we ask you to drink some water and rest for 30 minutes before checking again.

  2. Short questionnaire

    Before and after each session you tell us about your experience of that heating method.

  3. Core temperature probe

    You self-insert a thin, flexible, single-use probe 10 cm into the rectum, in a private locked cubicle. We explain exactly how beforehand, written instructions are in the cubicle and a researcher waits outside. It is the safest and most accurate way to track body temperature in the heat.

  4. Weighing

    We weigh you without clothes to measure sweat loss, in a private area with a screen and towel. A researcher of the same sex stays with you, watching mainly your face and posture, in case you feel faint.

  5. Sensors

    A heart rate strap around your chest, small temperature sensors taped to four places on your skin, a small sensor to measure skin blood flow, and an arm cuff for blood pressure.

  6. Heating

    Up to 45 minutes in the bath or sauna. In the saunas, you can step out for optional 5-minute breaks whenever you choose. Every 10 minutes we ask how hot and comfortable you feel and whether you have any symptoms.

  7. Recovery

    You are weighed again and rest in the lab until your core temperature is back within 0.5°C of where it started.

Before each visit, please

  • avoid strenuous exercise and alcohol for 24 hours
  • avoid caffeine for 12 hours
  • drink around 500 mL of water in the hour before you arrive
  • eat a similar light meal 2–3 hours before
  • avoid saunas, hot baths and hot-climate travel for the whole study
  • bring a pair of shorts or swimwear to wear during heating

Can I take part?

We aim to recruit 30 women and men across the full age range, because most heat research so far has focused on young men.

You may be able to take part if you

  • are aged 18–65 and in good general health
  • do not smoke
  • are a woman who has regular periods (every 21–35 days) and is willing to track your cycle, has used the same hormonal contraceptive for at least 3 months, or is postmenopausal (no periods for 12 months or more)

You will not be able to take part if you

  • have had heat stroke or serious heat illness
  • have a heart, lung or metabolic condition, such as diabetes
  • take medication that affects how your body controls its temperature, such as some anti-inflammatories, antidepressants, beta-blockers, statins or water tablets
  • have regularly used saunas or hot baths for heat training, or travelled to a hot climate, in the last 3 months
  • have a condition affecting the rectum (such as haemorrhoids, fissures or recent surgery) or a gastrointestinal infection
  • are claustrophobic
  • are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • are going through the menopause (perimenopause)
  • have used your current hormonal contraceptive for less than 3 months, or recently changed it

What you may feel

Heating is meant to be challenging. You may feel hot and sweaty, mildly tired, or get a headache. Some people feel light-headed, especially when standing up afterwards.

The rectal probe may cause mild discomfort when you insert it, and being weighed without clothes may feel awkward. Both happen in private, and we explain everything beforehand.

Four visits also take some of your time.

How we keep you safe

  • Heating stops if your body temperature reaches a set safety limit, or if you show any sign of heat illness.
  • You can stop at any time, without giving a reason. In the saunas, you can also step out for short breaks.
  • Two trained researchers are with you at every visit, and at least one is first aid trained.
  • Cooling methods are ready if you need them.
  • We help you out of the bath or sauna and you sit before standing.
  • You only leave once your temperature is back near your starting value.

Your information

The University of Brighton is the Data Controller for this study and manages research data in line with ethical requirements and UK data protection law.

This website and the interest form

This page is a static website hosted on GitHub Pages. It does not collect, store or process any information about you, and it uses no cookies or tracking.

If you register interest, you go to a University of Brighton Microsoft Form that asks only for your name, email address and confirmation that you are aged 18–65. It does not ask for any health information. Registering is not consent and does not confirm eligibility.

Participant ID numbers

If you take part, you get a unique ID number and everything we collect is recorded under that number, not your name. The list linking numbers to names is kept separately and only the main researcher can see it. It is destroyed after your final visit, after which your data is fully anonymous.

Storage and access

Electronic data is stored on the University’s secure OneDrive, on password-protected devices with two-factor authentication. Paper documents such as consent forms are kept in a locked cabinet, separately from research data. Your data is never stored on personal devices or external cloud services, and is never entered into any artificial intelligence (AI) platform. Only the researcher and supervisory team can access it.

Changing your mind

Taking part is entirely voluntary. You can stop or withdraw at any time without giving a reason, and no further data will be collected. Your rights to access, change or move information already collected are limited, so that the research stays reliable. Deciding not to take part will not affect any relationship you have with the University.

How long data is kept

Consent forms are kept until the PhD is awarded and then securely destroyed. Anonymised research data is kept for 10 years in the University of Brighton Research Data Repository, with restricted access. Your contact details are kept only if you ask for the findings, and deleted once these are sent.

What happens to the results

This is the first of three studies in a PhD. Results will appear in the thesis, and we aim to publish them in a scientific journal and present them at conferences. Results are reported for the group only, so you will not be identifiable.

Read more in the University research privacy notice and the Participant Information Sheet (PDF).

Questions

Do I have to take part?

No. It is entirely your choice. If you take part, you can stop at any time without giving a reason and without any consequences.

What happens after I register interest?

Gabriel will email you the Participant Information Sheet and answer any questions. Take as long as you need to decide. If you go ahead, you sign the consent form and complete the health questionnaire at your first visit.

Will I be paid?

No, taking part is unpaid. Parking at Falmer Campus is free, and we will make each visit as comfortable and convenient as possible.

Do I need to be fit or sporty?

No. This study involves no exercise. You sit still in the bath or sauna, and we want people of all ages and activity levels, not just athletes.

What should I bring?

A pair of shorts or swimwear to wear during heating. Towels are provided.

Can I see my results?

Yes, if you would like. You can receive your own results from each session and a short summary of the overall findings. These are for interest only and are not a medical assessment.
